;; life.txt — relations about organisms: kinship (parentOf/childOf/siblingOf/
;; ancestorOf/grandparentOf), age (birthDate/birthYearOf/olderThan/mortal), and behaviour
;; (eats, and the capabilityType/hasCapability pair, whose second argument is a
;; happening kind such as abstract.txt's flying or travelling). Loaded into CxUniverse with
;; the rest of kb/upper/.

(comment alive
         "(alive ?organism) means that ?organism is currently living. True by default of every organism and blocked for one known to be dead, so a KB never told otherwise takes its organisms to be alive — which is the common-sense reading, and the one every story assumes without saying.")
(unary_predicate alive)
(set/monotonic (genl alive organism))
(set/monotonic (stable_once_false alive))

(comment ancestorOf

(comment asleep
         "(asleep ?animal) means that ?animal is sleeping. Stated rather than derived, and what blocks the awake default; a rule concludes (not (awake ?x)) from it, so the two cannot both be believed of one animal and neither has to be retracted for the other to be told.")

(comment awake
         "(awake ?animal) means that ?animal is awake and able to act. True by default of an animal and blocked for one known to be asleep — the same shape as flight and penguins, on a state that changes rather than a species that does not.")
(unary_predicate awake)

(comment birthDate
         "(birthDate ?organism ?date) means that ?organism was born within ?date, a calendar term naming the birth at whatever precision is known: a year as (YearFn 1990), a month as (MonthFn 1990 5), a day as (DayFn 1990 5 3), or a moment as (InstantFn 1990 5 3 14 0 0). A tree or a bacterium is born as an animal is. Every coarser term holding the birth is a true birthDate as well, so the day of one birth and its year are both its birthDate.")
(implementationNote birthDate "Each rule below concludes the next coarser birthDate, from an InstantFn to its DayFn, a DayFn to its MonthFn and a MonthFn to its YearFn, and one rule concludes birthYearOf from the YearFn, so one rule alone concludes the functional birthYearOf. Two birth dates in different years clash on birthYearOf; two different days in one year clash with nothing, since birthDate is not functional. No rule reads the year out of a DatetimeFn or DatetimeRangeFn term, whose year is inside a string. The converse rule, from birthYearOf to the YearFn, is inert, so the clash on birthYearOf runs through the facts and not through a pair of rules concluding each other.")
(positiveExampleComment birthDate "(birthDate Tom (DayFn 1970 3 14)) beside (birthDate Tom (YearFn 1970))")
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate birthDate))
(generic_kinship_predicate birthDate)
(arg birthDate 1 organism)
(arg birthDate 2 uninterrupted_time)
;; Not functional: a birth stated to the day and to the year has two true birthDate
;; values, the day lying within the year.
(not (functional birthDate))
(termsRelated birthDate birthYearOf)

;; birthYearOf is a birthDate at the precision of a year.  Each finer birthDate concludes
;; the next coarser one, and the year's concludes birthYearOf.  The implementationNote on
;; birthDate says why the converse is inert.
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (birthDate ?organism (InstantFn ?year ?month ?day ?hour ?minute ?second))) (birthDate ?organism (DayFn ?year ?month ?day))))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (birthDate ?organism (DayFn ?year ?month ?day))) (birthDate ?organism (MonthFn ?year ?month))))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (birthDate ?organism (MonthFn ?year ?month))) (birthDate ?organism (YearFn ?year))))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (birthDate ?organism (YearFn ?year))) (birthYearOf ?organism ?year)))
(set/inertRule (implies (and (birthYearOf ?organism ?year)) (birthDate ?organism (YearFn ?year))))

(comment birthYearOf
         "(birthYearOf ?organism ?year) means that ?organism was born in ?year — a tree or a bacterium as well as an animal. It states what (birthDate ?organism (YearFn ?year)) states, and the rules beside birthDate conclude it from a birthDate stated at any calendar precision. The converse rule is inert, so a birthYearOf fact answers no birthDate goal. ?year is a plain integer rather than a term, so its arg is integer. Declared functional: a second, different year for one organism is a contradiction rather than a second fact, and that is what makes birthYearOf sound enough to derive olderThan from.")
(implementationNote birthYearOf "A number is typed by its kind, so (arg birthYearOf 2 integer) derives no membership. A value of another kind convicts the fact: assert stores it inert, never believed, and try-assert refuses it.")
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate birthYearOf))
(arg birthYearOf 1 organism)
(arg birthYearOf 2 integer)
(set/monotonic (functional birthYearOf))
(generic_kinship_predicate birthYearOf)

(comment eats
         "(eats ?animal ?food) means that ?animal takes ?food as nourishment. (comment FatherFn
         "(FatherFn ?animal) denotes the blood father of ?animal when its blood-parentage reading determines one.")

(comment capabilityType
         "(capabilityType ?kind ?happeningKind) means that a ?kind can be the doer of a happening of kind ?happeningKind. The type-level reading of hasCapability: (capabilityType bird flying) says the kind flies, where (hasCapability Tweety flying) says one bird does. Two predicates rather than one because they are two claims — a claim about a kind and a claim about a member — and one symbol read at both levels has to declare one position's argument check for both, which is a choice that mistypes whichever half it was not written for. Declared transitiveInArgInverse along genl on the FIRST position, which carries a claim about a kind down to its subkinds: a kind of bird flies with nothing written about it. Declared transitiveInArg along genl on the second, which carries it UP the genl hierarchy of happening kinds: flying is a kind of travelling, so a kind that can fly can travel. Paired with hasCapability, and the pairing moves no claim between the two: going from the kind to its members needs a quantifier reading (every bird, or some bird) that nothing here fixes, so the descent is a rule written where the kinds are. The comment below hasCapability says why that pairing is prose rather than a typeToInstancePred sentex.")
(set/monotonic (type_relation_predicate capabilityType))
(set/monotonic (time_agnostic_relation capabilityType))
(not (eternal_predicate capabilityType))
(genlArg capabilityType 1 animal)
(genlArg capabilityType 2 happening)
(set/monotonic (transitiveInArgInverse capabilityType 1 genl))
(transitiveInArg capabilityType 2 genl)

(comment hasCapability
         "(hasCapability ?doer ?happeningKind) means that ?doer can be the doer of a happening of kind ?happeningKind, the doer being the one doneBy names. The first position holds anything causal that can be such a doer: an animal, a machine, an organization. An instrument is not thereby a doer: a pencil is used in writing and does not write, so being usable in a kind of event is not a capability. The instance-level reading: (hasCapability Tweety flying) says this bird can fly and says nothing about birds, where the claim about the kind is capabilityType. The descent from the one to the other is a rule written where the kinds are, never an inference from the pairing. Carries no relation_kind because it is MIXED — the first position holds one causal thing and takes arg, the second names a happening KIND and takes genlArg — and the metatype classifies a predicate whose two ends sit at one level, so result and functionCorrespondingPredicate are left unmarked for the same reason. Declared transitiveInArg along genl on the second position, which carries a claim up the genl hierarchy of happening kinds: flying is a kind of travelling, so whatever can fly can travel.")
(binary_predicate hasCapability)
(arg hasCapability 1 causal)
(set/monotonic (time_varying_relation hasCapability))
(genlArg hasCapability 2 happening)
(transitiveInArg hasCapability 2 genl)

;; The pairing is stated in both comments above and NOT as (typeToInstancePred
;; capabilityType hasCapability): that predicate types its second argument an
;; instance_relation_predicate, so the link would derive that mark of hasCapability,
;; which carries no relation_kind because it is mixed.  So the one link that records a
;; type/instance pair cannot record a pair whose instance half is mixed without
;; mislabelling it, to gain a link the engine reads nothing from (vocabulary/classification
;; calls it inert), and the pairing is prose.

(comment mortal
         "(mortal ?organism) means that ?organism will die. Asserted as a default of every organism, so it is defeasible: something known not to die defeats it instead of contradicting the KB.")
(unary_predicate mortal)
(set/monotonic (time_invariant_relation mortal))

;; A dead dog is an animal that is not alive, and a living oak is alive and no animal.
;; dead and mortal cut across animal the same way, e.g. a hydra is an animal that is not
;; mortal.
(orthogonal animal mortal)
(orthogonal animal dead)
(orthogonal animal alive)
