;; SocietyContext — social relations among persons: likes, marriedTo, owns.
;; Sees CoreContext; UniverseContext sees it, so any relation here is visible
;; from every context.

(genlContext SocietyContext CoreContext)
(genlContext UniverseContext SocietyContext)

(comment friendOf
         "(friendOf ?person1 ?person2) means that ?person1 and ?person2 are friends. Declared symmetric, so one assertion answers both orders and the mirrored spelling stores as the same sentex. A rule carries it to knows, so friendship entails acquaintance and never the other way about.")
(binaryPredicate friendOf)
(instanceRelationPredicate friendOf)
(argIsa friendOf 1 person)
(argIsa friendOf 2 person)
(symmetric friendOf)

(comment knows
         "(knows ?person1 ?person2) means that ?person1 is acquainted with ?person2. Deliberately NOT symmetric: a great many people know someone who does not know them, and declaring the symmetry would make the KB assert that back. Derived from friendship, marriage and parenthood by rules, so most knows facts are consequences rather than claims anyone wrote.")
(binaryPredicate knows)
(instanceRelationPredicate knows)
(argIsa knows 1 person)
(argIsa knows 2 person)

(comment likes
         "(likes ?person ?thing) means that ?person is fond of ?thing. The first argument is a person; the second is anything at all, which is why it carries no argIsa — a person may like an object, a kind, or another person. An attitude, not a behaviour: nothing follows about what the person does.")
(binaryPredicate likes)
(instanceRelationPredicate likes)
(argIsa likes 1 person)

(comment marriedTo
         "(marriedTo ?person1 ?person2) means that ?person1 and ?person2 are married to each other. Declared symmetric, so one assertion answers both orders and the mirrored spelling stores as the same sentex. A standing state rather than the wedding: there is no time argument, so a KB that needs to say when it began states that of an interval instead. NOT decontextualized: a marriage is scoped to the context it is stated in, because marriages are temporally and jurisdictionally bounded — decontextualizing marriedTo causes temporal leakage when multiple marriages are stated in different temporal contexts.")
(binaryPredicate marriedTo)
(instanceRelationPredicate marriedTo)
(argIsa marriedTo 1 person)
(argIsa marriedTo 2 person)
(symmetric marriedTo)

(comment memberOf
         "(memberOf ?person ?organization) means that ?person belongs to ?organization. Membership in a body of people, which is not parthood: a member is not a component of the organization the way a wheel is of a car, and no mereological rule reaches it.")
(binaryPredicate memberOf)
(instanceRelationPredicate memberOf)
(argIsa memberOf 1 person)
(argIsa memberOf 2 organization)

(comment owns
         "(owns ?person ?object) means that ?person owns ?object. The second argument is a physical object; ownership distributes over its parts by a joined rule, so owning a whole entails owning each part and a great many owns facts are derived rather than asserted. Legal ownership rather than possession: nothing here says who is holding the thing.")
(binaryPredicate owns)
(instanceRelationPredicate owns)
(argIsa owns 1 person)
(argIsa owns 2 physical_object)

(comment speaks
         "(speaks ?person ?language) means that ?person speaks ?language. Says nothing about fluency and nothing about which language they speak best, and a person may speak several.")
(binaryPredicate speaks)
(instanceRelationPredicate speaks)
(argIsa speaks 1 person)
(argIsa speaks 2 language)

(comment worksFor
         "(worksFor ?person ?organization) means that ?person is employed by ?organization. A rule concludes membership from it, employment being one way of belonging to a body of people; the converse does not follow, since a member of a club is not employed by it.")
(binaryPredicate worksFor)
(instanceRelationPredicate worksFor)
(argIsa worksFor 1 person)
(argIsa worksFor 2 organization)
