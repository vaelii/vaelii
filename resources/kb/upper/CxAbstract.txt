;; CxAbstract — non-biological upper-ontology types (intangible, physical
;; objects, attributes, substances, artifacts, body parts and their kinds), the
;; physical-structure relations partOf/locatedIn/madeOf, and the two type-level
;; relations largerThan/partType. Sees CxCore; CxUniverse sees it, so any
;; type or relation here is visible from every context.

(genlCx CxAbstract CxCore)
(genlCx CxUniverse CxAbstract)

(comment artifact "A physical object made by an agent for a purpose.")
(genl artifact physical_object)

(comment attribute "A property an object can bear (a color, a size).")
(genl attribute intangible)

(comment body_part "A physical part of a living thing (a wing, a heart).")
(genl body_part physical_object)

(comment building "An artifact that is a fixed structure enclosing space.")
(genl building artifact)

(comment capability "Something a kind of thing can do — flying, or travelling. A noun for an ability, so that what a kind can do is a term the KB relates it to rather than a verb-shaped predicate of its own.")
(genl capability intangible)

(comment character_string "A run of text. The type is stated so a text-valued argument position can name what it holds; no string carries a type membership, so nothing is ever checked against it.")
(genl character_string intangible)
;; Text and a relation are both intangible and are not each other, and saying so is
;; what lets a rule be refused for feeding a text-valued argument into a position that
;; holds a type: every type is a unaryPredicate, so the type position asks for a
;; predicate, and a variable cannot be both at once.
(disjoint character_string predicate)

(comment largerThan
         "(largerThan ?kind1 ?kind2) means that a ?kind1 is bigger than a ?kind2. A claim about two KINDS rather than about two things — a typeRelationPredicate — so (largerThan dog cat) says dogs are bigger than cats and says nothing whatever about any particular dog. Transitive and asymmetric, so a cycle of size claims is a contradiction the KB reports rather than a chain it walks. Declared transitiveInArg on both positions along genl, which is what carries a claim down to subkinds: a kind of dog is larger than a kind of cat. That distribution is itself a claim about largerThan, which is why it is declared rather than assumed — the same inheritance is false of a chihuahua and a maine coon, and nothing in a sentence says which case it is in.")
(binaryPredicate largerThan)
(typeRelationPredicate largerThan)
(genlArg largerThan 1 physical_object)
(genlArg largerThan 2 physical_object)
(transitive largerThan)
(asymmetric largerThan)
(transitiveInArg largerThan 1 genl)
(transitiveInArg largerThan 2 genl)

(comment leaf "A flat green body part a plant photosynthesizes with.")
(genl leaf body_part)

(comment leg "A body part an animal stands and walks on.")
(genl leg body_part)

(comment liquid "A substance that flows and takes the shape of its container.")
(genl liquid substance)

(comment living_thing "An organism — it is born, lives, and (by default) dies.")
(genl living_thing physical_object)
(disjoint living_thing artifact)

(comment locatedIn
         "(locatedIn ?object ?place) means that ?object sits within ?place. Declared transitive, so a chain of containments is walked by the closure prover rather than materialized, and a joined rule carries location down parthood — a part is located wherever its whole is. Both arguments are physical objects: a region is CxSpace's business, and partOfRegion is the relation to reach for there.")
(binaryPredicate locatedIn)
(instanceRelationPredicate locatedIn)
(arg locatedIn 1 physical_object)
(arg locatedIn 2 physical_object)
(transitive locatedIn)

(comment machine "An artifact with moving parts that does work under power.")
(genl machine artifact)

(comment madeOf
         "(madeOf ?object ?substance) means that ?object is made of ?substance. About one particular object and the stuff it is made of, so it is instance-level; what a whole kind is made of is a claim about that kind. Partial rather than exhaustive — a thing may be made of several substances, and saying it is made of one does not deny the others.")
(binaryPredicate madeOf)
(instanceRelationPredicate madeOf)
(arg madeOf 1 physical_object)
(arg madeOf 2 substance)

(comment metal "A hard, dense, conducting substance — iron, copper, gold.")
(genl metal substance)

(comment organization "A group of people acting as one body — a company, a school, a team.")
(genl organization intangible)

(comment partOf
         "(partOf ?part ?whole) means that ?part is a component of ?whole. Declared transitive, so a part of a part is a part and the chain is answered by the closure prover rather than stored. Structural parthood between physical objects — not membership in a collection, and not the spatial containment partOfRegion states of regions.")
(binaryPredicate partOf)
(instanceRelationPredicate partOf)
(arg partOf 1 physical_object)
(arg partOf 2 physical_object)
(transitive partOf)

(comment partType
         "(partType ?wholeKind ?partKind) means that a ?wholeKind has a ?partKind among its parts. The type-level reading of partOf: (partType bird wing) says birds have wings, where (partOf LeftWing Pingu) says one thing is part of another. Declared transitiveInArg along genl on the FIRST position only, and the asymmetry is the claim: a kind of bird has whatever parts birds have, so penguins have wings without a word being written about penguins, while the second position preserves nothing — birds having wings says nothing about which kinds of wing they have. Linked to partOf by typeToInstancePred, which records that the two are readings of one relation and deliberately moves no claim between them: going from the kinds to their members needs a quantifier reading (every bird has SOME wing) that nothing here fixes, and guessing it is how a claim about kinds quietly becomes a false claim about individuals.")
(binaryPredicate partType)
(typeRelationPredicate partType)
(genlArg partType 1 physical_object)
(genlArg partType 2 physical_object)
(transitiveInArg partType 1 genl)
(typeToInstancePred partType partOf)
