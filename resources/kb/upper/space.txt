;; space.txt — qualitative spatial relations. Four independent calculi: the RCC-8
;; TOPOLOGY of regions (do they touch, overlap, nest?), the cardinal DIRECTION of one
;; place from another (which way, and how far round the compass?), RELATIVE DIRECTION
;; (which way is one thing from another as seen from a point of view?) and qualitative
;; DISTANCE (how far apart, on a scale of classes rather than in numbers?). Loaded into
;; CxUniverse with the rest of kb/upper/.
;;
;; Topology. Eight BASE relations, jointly exhaustive and pairwise disjoint, so exactly
;; one holds of any two regions: spatiallyDisconnected (DC), externallyConnected (EC),
;; partiallyOverlapping (PO), spatiallyEqual (EQ), tangentialProperPart (TPP),
;; nonTangentialProperPart (NTPP) and the two inverses (TPPi, NTPPi). Six DERIVED
;; relations each name a disjunction of them: regionConnectedTo (C), partOfRegion (P),
;; properPartOfRegion (PP), hasRegionPart (Pi), regionOverlaps (O), regionDiscreteFrom
;; (DR).
;;
;; Direction. Nine BASE relations, again jointly exhaustive and pairwise disjoint: the
;; eight compass points (northOf, northeastOf, eastOf, southeastOf, southOf, southwestOf,
;; westOf, northwestOf) and sameLocationAs for two places that coincide. Four DERIVED
;; relations name one axis and leave the other open — northwardOf, southwardOf,
;; eastwardOf, westwardOf — so northwardOf covers north, northeast and northwest alike.
;;
;; Relative direction. The same nine-way shape over a frame's own axes rather than the
;; compass: leftOf, rightOf, inFrontOf, behind, the four corners between them
;; (frontLeftOf, frontRightOf, behindLeftOf, behindRightOf) and sameRelativePositionAs.
;; Four DERIVED relations again name one axis and leave the other open — leftwardOf,
;; rightwardOf, frontwardOf, rearwardOf. The FRAME OF REFERENCE IS THE CONTEXT: relative
;; direction is ternary in the literature (A is left of B from viewpoint C), and a
;; constraint network is binary, so the viewpoint is the context the facts are stated
;; in. (leftOf Mouse Lion) in CxLionMouse is a claim in that frame and in no other;
;; two contexts looking from opposite sides state opposite facts and neither contaminates
;; the other. Nothing declares a viewpoint, because the context is the viewpoint.
;;
;; Distance. Seven BASE relations, an ordered chain of classes covering every distance
;; exactly once: coLocatedWith (zero), veryCloseTo, closeTo, nearTo, moderatelyFarFrom,
;; farFrom, veryFarFrom. Three DERIVED relations name a range of the chain —
;; withinNearDistanceOf (no further than near), beyondFarDistanceFrom (at least far) and
;; atSomeDistanceFrom (any distance above zero). Distance is symmetric, so each of these
;; reads the same both ways round. The chain composes by the triangle inequality, which
;; is weak: two mid-range classes usually compose to several, so this calculus earns its
;; keep by ruling arrangements out — two things very close to a third cannot be very far
;; from each other — rather than by fixing a distance.
;;
;; All of them are ordinary stored facts. A prover reads every relation of its own
;; calculus visible in a context into a single constraint network, tightens it by
;; composition, and answers a goal by entailment — (P a b) holds iff every relation still
;; possible between a and b satisfies P. So (nonTangentialProperPart A B) and
;; (nonTangentialProperPart B C) answer (nonTangentialProperPart A C), and the weaker
;; (partOfRegion A C) with it; (northeastOf A B) and (northeastOf B C) answer
;; (northeastOf A C), and the weaker (northwardOf A C) and (eastwardOf A C) with it; and
;; (leftOf A B) with (leftOf B C) answers (leftOf A C) in the frame all three were stated
;; in; and (coLocatedWith A B) with (closeTo B C) answers (closeTo A C). Each prover is
;; opt-in; the vocabulary is not. Every argument position here is declared at
;; spatial — located in some space, the world or a mathematical one such as a plane —
;; and nothing narrower, since none of the four calculi needs time; picking them out
;; more finely is not this file's job. The context keeps two spaces apart, as it keeps
;; two frames of reference apart. See docs/space.md.
;;
;; Each converse pair is stated inert, as (set/inert (inverse P Q)), so the pair is
;; written down where a reader looks. The algebra computes every converse itself, so a
;; believed copy adds no answer, and the calculus prover still answers a goal alone.

(comment atSomeDistanceFrom
         "(atSomeDistanceFrom ?thing1 ?thing2) means that ?thing1 and ?thing2 are some distance apart. Any class of the distance chain but the zero one, so it is the complement of coLocatedWith and holds of nothing and itself.")
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate atSomeDistanceFrom))
(set/monotonic (time_agnostic_relation atSomeDistanceFrom))
(arg atSomeDistanceFrom 1 spatial)
(arg atSomeDistanceFrom 2 spatial)
