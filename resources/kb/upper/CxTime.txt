;; CxTime — the theory of time: qualitative relations between INTERVALS in Allen's
;; algebra, qualitative order between INSTANTS in the point algebra, the two joined by an
;; interval's endpoints, and the metric layer that puts real measures on the gaps. Sees
;; CxCore; CxUniverse sees it, so a temporal relation is stateable from every
;; context. An interval and an instant are both ordinary individuals — nothing here
;; declares them.
;;
;; The three calendar constructors are the exception, and they name intervals rather than
;; ordering them: (YearFn 2000), (MonthFn 2000 1) and (DayFn 2000 1 15) denote the year,
;; the month and the day as stretches of time. Each is an unreifiable_function, so its
;; fields stay readable inside the term, which is what lets a reader see that January 2000
;; sits inside 2000 without anybody stating it — the containment a time-keyed CONTEXT is
;; ordered by (docs/context-nat.md). InstantFn is the clock beside them: it names a MOMENT
;; by its six calendar fields, so (InstantFn 2000 1 1 0 0 0) is midnight at the start of
;; 2000, and startOf and endOf are answered for a calendar term by computing those two
;; moments rather than by anybody stating them. Half-open: a term's end is the first
;; moment of the next term at the same precision, so the end of 1999 and the start of 2000
;; are one term and consecutive calendar terms meet. Nothing is stored — the prover is
;; opt-in, like every other one here. See docs/time.md.
;;
;; Thirteen BASE relations, jointly exhaustive and pairwise disjoint, so exactly one holds
;; of any two intervals. Writing an interval as its start and end: before (A ends before B
;; starts), meets (A ends exactly where B starts), overlaps (A starts first and they share
;; a middle), finishedBy / contains / startedBy (A extends past B on one or both sides),
;; starts / during / finishes (B extends past A on one or both sides), intervalEqual (same
;; start, same end), and the converses overlappedBy, metBy and after. Six converse pairs
;; and one self-converse: before/after, meets/metBy, overlaps/overlappedBy,
;; starts/startedBy, during/contains, finishes/finishedBy, and intervalEqual.
;;
;; Seven DERIVED relations each name a disjunction of them: precedes (before or meets) and
;; its converse precededBy, for an ordering that does not care whether the intervals
;; touch; subintervalOf (during, starts, finishes or intervalEqual), properSubintervalOf
;; (the same without intervalEqual) and hasSubinterval (its converse), for temporal
;; containment; and the complementary pair sharesTimeWith (the nine relations under which
;; two intervals share some time) and temporallyDisjoint (the four under which they do
;; not).
;;
;; intervalEqual is a claim about extent in time, not about the identity of two terms, so
;; it is not the equality closure's equals: two distinct meetings can run start to finish
;; together without being one meeting.
;;
;; Instants are the other unit, and the smaller one: a moment has no extent, so the only
;; question about two of them is which came first. Three BASE relations, again jointly
;; exhaustive and pairwise disjoint — instantBefore, instantEqual, instantAfter — and three
;; DERIVED ones, each the complement of one of them and so, over a jointly-exhaustive
;; triple, literally its negation: instantNotAfter (at or before, the ≤ of time),
;; instantNotBefore (at or after, the ≥) and instantNotEqual. Every name carries the
;; instant prefix because before and after already belong to the intervals, and a moment
;; ordered against a moment is a different claim from a stretch ordered against a stretch.
;;
;; startOf and endOf join the two: they name an interval's two bounding instants, so an
;; Allen relation between two intervals and an ordering between their endpoints are claims
;; about the same thing. That is also where the metric layer attaches. temporalDistance
;; states how far apart two instants are, as a measure — an exact (QuantityFn N Unit) or a
;; bounded (QuantityIntervalFn Lo Hi Unit), and negative when the second instant comes
;; first. A prover closes those constraints by all-pairs shortest paths, answers the
;; tightest separation entailed between any two instants, and reports a network that
;; contradicts itself. See docs/stp.md.
;;
;; Duration is the quantitative half, and it is where time meets measurement. An
;; interval's own duration is the stored fact (length I M), whose measure M is one of
;; CxMeasure's structural NATs. totalDuration and overlapDuration are computed from those and
;; never stored: a prover reads the lengths, normalizes them against the unit table, does
;; the arithmetic on magnitude bounds, and renders the answer in the dimension's base
;; unit — as a point when it is exact and an interval when it is only bounded, so an
;; over-approximation says so rather than claiming a figure. See docs/duration.md.
;;
;; Events and fluents are the other thing time is for, and they are what changes: a
;; fluent is a state of affairs that holds over some stretches and not others, an event
;; happens at an instant, and initiates and terminates say which fluents that event starts
;; and stops. holdsAt asks whether a fluent is the case at a moment, and clipped and
;; clippedBefore say when one was ended in between. Only the first four are ever stated —
;; the last three are what CxChange's inertia axioms derive from them, so this file
;; declares the vocabulary and the theory that reasons with it lives one band down. See
;; docs/time.md.
;;
;; All of them are ordinary stored facts. A prover reads every interval relation visible
;; in a context into a single constraint network, tightens it by composition, and answers
;; a goal by entailment — (P a b) holds iff every relation still possible between a and b
;; satisfies P. So (before A B) and (before B C) answer (before A C), and (during A B)
;; with (during B C) answers (during A C) and the weaker (subintervalOf A C) with it. The
;; prover is opt-in; the vocabulary is not. See docs/time.md.
