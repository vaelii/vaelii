(comment body_part "A part an organism grew — a wing, a heart, a leaf. It stays a body part once transplanted into another organism or shed from its own.")
;; An organism and a part it grew are both biological and never the same thing; nothing
;; says every biological thing is one of the two.  The roster installs each part's genl
;; edge to biological; core.txt states organism's as a sentence too.
(separating biological organism body_part)
;; A leg of lamb or a chicken wing is food and a body part at once.
(orthogonal food body_part)
;; An orthogonal is not inherited along genl, so each overlapping pair is stated or
;; derived.  origin_type classifies a tangible by where its form came from, and each
;; typeOrthogonal fact below makes made and natural both orthogonal to one type.  A cloned
;; sheep is a made organism and a wild sheep a natural one.  A lab-grown bladder is a made
;; body part and a heart a natural one.  Steel, water and sand are a made, a natural and a
;; formation substance.  Bread, an apple and sea salt are a made, a natural and a
;; formation food, and an apple is biological food.  An engineered bacterium is made and
;; biological.
(comment origin_type "A type of tangible by where its form came from: made or natural.")
(metatype origin_type)
(typeGenl origin_type tangible)
;; Each membership is definitional, so origin_type is on the forced-monotonic roster and
;; typeOrthogonal's rule concludes an orthogonal from one alone (docs/nmtms.md).
(forced_monotonic_predicate origin_type)
(set/monotonic (time_invariant_relation origin_type))
(origin_type made)
(origin_type natural)
(partitionedByType tangible origin_type)
(typeOrthogonal origin_type organism)
(typeOrthogonal origin_type animal)
(typeOrthogonal origin_type body_part)
(typeOrthogonal origin_type substance)
(typeOrthogonal origin_type food)
(typeOrthogonal origin_type biological)
;; A bonsai or a grafted apple tree is a made plant, and a rock is natural and no plant.
(typeOrthogonal origin_type plant)
;; A cloned sheep is a made vertebrate, and a lab-bred fruit fly a made invertebrate.
(orthogonal made vertebrate)
(orthogonal made invertebrate)
(orthogonal substance formation)
(orthogonal food biological)
(orthogonal food formation)
;; A steel ingot is a made solid, and a granite boulder a natural one.
(orthogonal made solid)
;; A bladder is a biological container, and a cup a container that is not biological.
(orthogonal biological container)

(comment typeLargerThan
         "(typeLargerThan ?kind1 ?kind2) means that a ?kind1 is bigger than a ?kind2. A claim about two KINDS rather than about two things — a type_relation_predicate — so (typeLargerThan dog cat) says dogs are bigger than cats and says nothing whatever about any particular dog. Transitive and asymmetric, so a cycle of size claims is a contradiction the KB reports rather than a chain it walks. Declared transitiveInArgInverse on both positions along genl, which is what carries a claim down to subkinds: a kind of dog is larger than a kind of cat. That distribution is itself a claim about typeLargerThan, which is why it is declared rather than assumed — the same inheritance is false of a chihuahua and a maine coon, and nothing in a sentence says which case it is in.")

(set/monotonic (type_relation_predicate typeLargerThan))

(set/monotonic (transitive typeLargerThan))
(set/monotonic (asymmetric typeLargerThan))
(set/monotonic (transitiveInArgInverse typeLargerThan 1 genl))
(set/monotonic (transitiveInArgInverse typeLargerThan 2 genl))

(comment partOf
         "(partOf ?part ?whole) means that ?part is a part of ?whole, of any kind. A component of an object, a region inside a region and a stage of a situation are all parts. Declared transitive, so a part of a part is a part. Both positions are untyped, because a part and its whole can be of any kind.")
(binary_predicate partOf)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate partOf))
(set/monotonic (transitive partOf))
(set/monotonic (reflexive partOf))
;; Antisymmetry was weighed and declined: two coextensive regions stay two regions, so
;; two things each part of the other are not thereby one.  anti_symmetric is on the
;; forced-monotonic roster, so the denial is stored and held OUT rather than believed.
(not (anti_symmetric partOf))

(comment properPartOf
         "(properPartOf ?part ?whole) means that ?part is a part of ?whole and ?whole is not a part of ?part, of any kind. Both positions are untyped, as partOf's are. Two things each a part of the other are neither a proper part of the other, and they stay two things.")
(binary_predicate properPartOf)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate properPartOf))
(set/monotonic (irreflexive properPartOf))
(set/monotonic (transitive properPartOf))
(genl properPartOf partOf)
(set/monotonic (properCounterpart partOf properPartOf))
;; A proper part's whole is not a part of it.  Stated once here, at the root: every proper
;; parthood relation is below properPartOf, and a denial of partOf denies each spec's tuple.
(set/forwardRule (implies (properPartOf ?part ?whole) (not (partOf ?whole ?part))))

(comment permanentPartOf
         "(permanentPartOf ?part ?whole) means that ?part is a part of ?whole that could not stop being one while both exist. A region's parts and a situation's parts are permanent parts. A wheel is a part of a car and no permanent part, since it can come off. Declared transitive, and both positions are untyped, as partOf's are.")
(binary_predicate permanentPartOf)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate permanentPartOf))
(set/monotonic (time_invariant_relation permanentPartOf))
(set/monotonic (transitive permanentPartOf))
(set/monotonic (reflexive permanentPartOf))
(genl permanentPartOf partOf)

(comment tangiblePartOf

Declared transitive, so a part of a part is a part and the chain is answered by the closure prover rather than stored. Structural parthood between physical objects — not membership in a collection, and not the spatial containment partOfRegion states of regions.")
(binary_predicate tangiblePartOf)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate tangiblePartOf))

(arg properTangiblePartOf 1 tangible)

(comment partType
         "(partType ?wholeKind ?partKind) means that a ?wholeKind has a ?partKind among its parts. The type-level reading of tangiblePartOf: (partType bird wing) says birds have wings, where (tangiblePartOf LeftWing Pingu) says one thing is part of another. Declared transitiveInArgInverse along genl on the FIRST position only, and the asymmetry is the claim: a kind of bird has whatever parts birds have, so penguins have wings without a word being written about penguins, while the second position preserves nothing — birds having wings says nothing about which kinds of wing they have. Linked to tangiblePartOf by typeToInstancePred, which records that the two are readings of one relation and deliberately moves no claim between them: going from the kinds to their members needs a quantifier reading (every bird has SOME wing) that nothing here fixes, and guessing it is how a claim about kinds quietly becomes a false claim about individuals.")
(binary_predicate partType)
(set/monotonic (type_relation_predicate partType))
(genlArg partType 1 tangible)
(genlArg partType 2 tangible)
(set/monotonic (transitiveInArgInverse partType 1 genl))
(typeToInstancePred partType tangiblePartOf)

(comment time "A time, as in \"at that time\": a moment, a stretch, or stretches with gaps between them, as such, not something that happens in it. In time and in no space, and never a cause: the year 2000 broke nothing, two-digit years did, at the rollover. Parent of uninterrupted_time and intermittent_time.")
;; core.txt states time's edges to temporal and aspatial, (partition time uninterrupted_time
;; intermittent_time), the intersections defining the two parts and (partition
;; uninterrupted_time time_point time_interval).
(genl time acausal)

(comment uninterrupted_time "A time with no gap: a moment, or one unbroken stretch of time. The intersection of uninterrupted and time. Parent of time_point (a moment) and time_interval (a stretch).")

(comment intermittent_time "A time with a gap: there is a moment between its start and its end that it does not include, as with the evenings of one week or the days of a trial. The intersection of intermittent and time.")

(comment time_point "A moment rather than a stretch — one of the two instants bounding an interval. What the point algebra relates, and what startOf and endOf reach from an interval.")

(comment time_interval "A stretch of time as such, e.g. a year, a day, an hour — not something that happens in it. What the calendar constructors YearFn, MonthFn and DayFn name.")

;; A time is not a state of affairs in it.
(set/monotonic (disjoint time situation))

;; --- situation / uninterrupted_situation / static_situation / event ------------
(comment situation "A state of affairs: objects bearing properties or relations over some time, with or without gaps. Parent of fluent (it holds) and happening (it happens), and of uninterrupted_situation (it has no gap). A situation is located in its region, not made of the matter in it, e.g. a boulder is an input to its rolling and not a part of it.")

(comment uninterrupted_situation "A situation with no gap in its time: the intersection of uninterrupted and situation. Parent of static_situation (the state holds unchanged) and event (the world's state changes); change is the specialization axis.")

(comment static_situation "A situation extended in time but unchanged, a held state, e.g. a file existing: the intersection of uninterrupted and fluent. Contrast event, the kind whose state changes.")

(comment event "Something that happens: an occurrence located in time, with no gap, whose state changes. The intersection of uninterrupted and happening. The event-calculus slots do not name it: happens takes a temporal thing, and initiates and terminates take a causal one.")
;; core.txt states (genl situation temporal), the partition of situation into fluent and
;; happening, and the intersections defining uninterrupted_situation, static_situation and
;; event.

(comment spatial_event "An event located in some space: a battle, a smelting, a sawing, a footprint's step. An institutional change, such as a contract expiring, is an event located nowhere and is not a spatial event.")
(intersection spatial_event event spatial)
;; A spatial event is in some space and, as an event, in time, so it is spatiotemporal.
;; spatiotemporal is below spatial and temporal by genl edges and is no intersection, so
;; nothing derives the edge, and it is stated.
(genl spatial_event spatiotemporal)

;; --- subSituationOf: a situation that is part of another ---------------------
(comment subSituationOf
         "(subSituationOf ?part ?whole) means that the situation ?part is a part of the situation ?whole. Declared transitive, so a part of a part is a part. A part's time is within its whole's time. The converse does not hold: two situations sharing a time are not thereby parts of one another. subSituationOf supplies parthood only. It rolls no role of the part up to the whole, and it closes no situation under the sum of its parts.")

(genl subSituationOf permanentPartOf)

(comment properSubSituationOf

(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate properSubSituationOf))
(arg properSubSituationOf 1 situation)

;; --- causal / acausal: whether a thing can occupy a cause slot -----------------
(comment causal "A thing that can be a cause, i.e. can occupy the cause slot of a causal relation. A cause is in time, so causal is below temporal. One side of the causal/acausal division of thing.")

(comment acausal "A thing that cannot be a cause: a time, a property line, an attribute, the information a record or piece of evidence carries (a reading of it can cause; the information itself causes nothing). The other side of the causal/acausal division of thing.")

;; core.txt states (partition thing causal acausal), (genl causal temporal) and (genl
;; atemporal acausal).  The partition also makes causal_event and acausal_event disjoint,
;; by the genl closure of disjointness.

;; Anything with mass can fill a cause slot: the rock dented the car. tangible is
;; core.txt's, and the edge is stated here beside causal's comment; with the partition it
;; also keeps every tangible out of acausal.
(genl tangible causal)

;; A body of people can be a cause too: a company hires; a court rules. organization
;; is aspatial, and nothing separates aspatial from causal.
(genl organization causal)

;; --- causal_event / acausal_event: events by whether they can cause ------------
;; defined via intersection; its rules derive the genls and membership.
(comment causal_event "An event that is a cause: the intersection of causal and event.")
(intersection causal_event causal event)

(comment acausal_event "An event that is not a cause (an observation or receipt is one): the intersection of acausal and event.")
(intersection acausal_event acausal event)

;; --- travelling / flying: event kinds an ability names ------------------------
;; life.txt's hasCapability and capabilityType take an event kind as their second argument:
;; (hasCapability Tweety flying) says Tweety can be the doer of a flying event.
(comment travelling "A causal event of getting from one place to another under the doer's own power. flying is a kind of travelling; another way of travelling is stated as its own event kind below travelling.")
(genl travelling causal_event)

(comment flying "A travelling event through the air under the doer's own power. A kind of travelling, so whatever can fly can travel without that being stated twice.")
(genl flying travelling)

;; --- causes: a causal thing brings a situation about ---------------------------
(comment causes
         "(causes ?cause ?effect) means that ?cause brings ?effect about: the flattery made the crow sing, the rock dented the car, the drought ruined the harvest. The cause is a causal thing: a causal event, a tangible or an organization. The effect is a situation: something that holds or happens, over any extent. Declared transitive, so a chain of causes is itself a cause and the chain is answered by the closure prover rather than stored.")
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate causes))
(arg causes 1 causal)
(arg causes 2 situation)
(set/monotonic (transitive causes))
;; A cause starts no later than its effect.  The rule concludes a point relation, which the
;; point network reads: with the point reasoner registered, a stated order that puts an
;; effect's start before its cause's is a qualitative inconsistency.  A cause may go on
;; after its effect starts.
(set/forwardRule
 (implies (and (causes ?cause ?effect))
          (instantNotAfter (StartFn ?cause) (StartFn ?effect))))

;; --- doneBy / performedBy: an event's doer -----------------------------------
(comment doneBy
         "(doneBy ?event ?doer) means that ?doer brought about ?event, intentionally or not. The doer can be any thing — a person, an animal, a machine, a process — so the second position declares no type: agent names a registered participant rather than something that can act, and no type below thing covers every doer. performedBy is the intentional case.")
(binary_predicate doneBy)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate doneBy))
(arg doneBy 1 event)

(comment performedBy
         "(performedBy ?event ?doer) means that ?doer intentionally initiated ?event. Specializes doneBy through a genl edge, so every performing is a doing and a doing is not concluded a performing.")
(binary_predicate performedBy)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate performedBy))
(arg performedBy 1 event)
(genl performedBy doneBy)

;; --- input / output: what went into an event and what it left behind -----------
;; Each relation takes the event first, as every relation over an event does.  Each states
;; binary_predicate beside instance_relation_predicate.  The arity check holds out a fact
;; of the wrong length only where binary_predicate is stated, and the genl edge from
;; instance_relation_predicate does not reach that check.  CxChange states when an input
;; and an output exist relative to their event, in time.txt's point relations.
(comment input "(input ?event ?thing) means that ?thing went into ?event: the lettuce in a salad, the ore in smelting, the plank in sawing. An instrument the event used and left unchanged, like the knife or the saw, is not an input. Contrast output.")
(binary_predicate input)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate input))
(arg input 1 event)
(termsRelated input output)

(comment destroyedInput "(destroyedInput ?event ?thing) means that ?thing went into ?event and ceased to exist there: the ore in smelting, the plank in sawing, the egg in an omelet. The lettuce in a salad is an input but not a destroyed one.")
(binary_predicate destroyedInput)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate destroyedInput))
(arg destroyedInput 1 event)
(genl destroyedInput input)

(comment preservedInput "(preservedInput ?event ?thing) means that ?thing went into ?event and still exists when ?event ends: the lettuce in a salad, the thread in a woven cloth.")
(binary_predicate preservedInput)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate preservedInput))
(arg preservedInput 1 event)
(genl preservedInput input)
;; No thing is both a destroyed and a preserved input of one event.  The KB does not
;; state the disjointness, because a separation over two relations constrains no tuple.

(comment output
         "(output ?event ?thing) means that ?event gave ?thing the form or content it has, as something it left behind: a chair from its making, sawdust from sawing, a footprint from a step, a file from a program run. Whether that was the point of the event does not matter.")
(binary_predicate output)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate output))
(arg output 1 event)

(comment tangibleOutput
         "(tangibleOutput ?event ?thing) means that ?thing is a tangible output of ?event: a chair from its making, steel from smelting, sawdust from sawing, a footprint from a step.")
(binary_predicate tangibleOutput)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate tangibleOutput))
(arg tangibleOutput 1 event)
(arg tangibleOutput 2 tangible)
(genl tangibleOutput output)

(comment intangibleOutput
         "(intangibleOutput ?event ?thing) means that ?thing is an intangible output of ?event: a file from a program run, a receipt from a tool call, a recorded measurement from an observation.")
(binary_predicate intangibleOutput)
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate intangibleOutput))
(arg intangibleOutput 1 event)
(arg intangibleOutput 2 intangible)
(genl intangibleOutput output)

;; A tangible output of an intentionally initiated event is made.  Growth is never
;; intentionally initiated, so a calf is not made by its mother.
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (tangibleOutput ?e ?x) (performedBy ?e ?d)) (made ?x)))
;; A tangible output of an event a made thing did is made: a widget a factory machine
;; stamped is made.
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (tangibleOutput ?e ?x) (doneBy ?e ?d) (made ?d)) (made ?x)))
