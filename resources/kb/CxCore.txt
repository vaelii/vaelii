;; CxCore — Vaelii's vocabulary context: the engine-interpreted special
;; predicates (types, contexts, rules, metadata, evaluables) and the predicate
;; meta-ontology, each documented via a comment sentex. Schema only — the domain
;; relations, individuals, and facts live in other contexts.
;;
;; The test for belonging here is **grammar, not subject matter**: a term earns a place
;; only if it is vocabulary several upper-spindle members state their content *in*.
;; binaryPredicate, argIsa, genl and unreifiableFunction are grammar — every domain
;; declares its relations, constraints, types and functions with them. dimensionOf and
;; partOfRegion are not: they are *about* something (measurement, space), so they live
;; in the one upper context that owns that subject (kb/upper/CxMeasure.txt,
;; kb/upper/CxSpace.txt). A term whose only user is a single upper context belongs
;; in that context, whatever interprets it — being read by a prover does not make a term
;; part of the grammar.
;;
;; What that buys is separability, not omission: the starter loads every upper context
;; it finds, but a KB assembled from CxCore plus the layers it wants carries only
;; the subjects it reasons about, and nothing here has to change to add or drop one.

(comment abduciblePredicate
         "(abduciblePredicate ?predicate) means that a (?predicate …) sentence may be hypothesized. When a backward proof dead-ends on one, abduce may assume it rather than fail. A grant, and the only thing that makes a predicate abducible: an undeclared one is never assumed, since an abducer that will hypothesize anything explains everything and is worth nothing. Read from the asking context's genlCx up-cone rather than universally, so it is a policy of the context that gives it — one theory may be willing to assume what another, reading the same vocabulary, will not.")
(unaryPredicate abduciblePredicate)

;; The meta-ontology rules below conclude into the ORDINARY placement — the maximal
;; context that sees the rule and the declaration — and not into a named one.  Every
;; context sees CxCore, so a declaration made here still concludes here; one made
;; in a context concludes there, where its author put it.  Naming CxCore would
;; publish it: a private (symmetric myRel) would become vocabulary the whole KB reads,
;; while (has-prop? kb :symmetric myRel ctx) — asked of the same declaration from the
;; same context — answered false.  One KB cannot say both.

;; arity concludes the predicate type
(implies (and (arity ?p 1)) (unaryPredicate ?p))
(implies (and (arity ?p 2)) (binaryPredicate ?p))
(implies (and (arity ?p 3)) (ternaryPredicate ?p))

;; the predicate type concludes the arity
(implies (and (unaryPredicate ?p))   (arity ?p 1))
(implies (and (binaryPredicate ?p))  (arity ?p 2))
(implies (and (ternaryPredicate ?p)) (arity ?p 3))

         "(transitive ?predicate) means that ?predicate is transitive, so (?predicate a b) and (?predicate b c) imply (?predicate a c). ;; genl and genlCx ARE transitive — but the engine answers them from its cached genl/genlCx
;; closures rather than the generic closure prover, which is an implementation choice and
;; not a difference in what they mean.  Saying so is what lets them be named as the relation
;; an argument position is preserved along, like any other transitive relation, and answers
;; a query for the transitive predicates with them in it.
;;
;; The declaration is stored but **inert**: the mark ingestion skips the two `closure-relations`
;; (genl, genlCx), so it never sets `has-prop? :transitive` on them and the generic prover —
;; which explicitly excludes the two it cannot beat — is never handed them.  `(transitive genl)`
;; is thus a queryable classification, not a routing.
;;
;; Stated HERE, below `(genl transitive binaryPredicate)`, and not up in the `genl` block: a
;; fact asserted before the edge that makes its predicate a subtype does not retroactively fire
;; the rules keyed on the supertype, so `(transitive genl)` written earlier would leave the arity
;; rule's firing latent — believed all the same, by the other justification, but produced on
;; whatever assert next joined the rule over the extent rather than at load.
(transitive genl)
(transitive genlCx)

(comment antiTransitive
         "(antiTransitive ?predicate) means that a two-step chain forbids the direct step, the dual of transitive: believing (?predicate a b) and (?predicate b c) makes (?predicate a c) contradictory. The chain conviction is DECLARED here and NOT yet enforced by the engine — a nogood over the direct step and the two chain steps is a three-party clash the settle machinery forms only pairwise (docs/nmtms.md), and a partial door-only check would decide the same three facts differently in different arrival orders, which the order-independence invariant forbids. What IS enforced is the classification and its disjointness with transitive: no predicate is declared both. Also a type: (antiTransitive P) classifies P as a binaryPredicate. Declared of a relation such as parentOf (a grandparent is not a parent), for instance.")
(unaryPredicate antiTransitive)
(decontextualizedPredicate antiTransitive)
(genl antiTransitive binaryPredicate)

(comment equivalenceRelation
         "(equivalenceRelation ?predicate) means that ?predicate is reflexive, symmetric and transitive at once — an equivalence. Declared once, it stands in for the three: forward rules derive (symmetric ?p), (transitive ?p) and (reflexive ?p), each a real mark the engine enforces in turn — the symmetric canonical-order fold, the transitive closure prover, the reflexive prover. A subsumption edge such as (genl equivalenceRelation symmetric) would classify P as symmetric for a query but would not set the :symmetric property the engine reads to enforce, since a genl-inherited membership is not a stored (symmetric P) sentex and never reaches the mark ingestion; the rule materializes that sentex, which both classifies and enforces, so the rules are the minimal correct expression and the subsumption edges would be redundant with them. Also a type: (equivalenceRelation P) classifies P as a binaryPredicate. Declared of a relation such as sameAgeAs, for instance.")
(unaryPredicate equivalenceRelation)
(decontextualizedPredicate equivalenceRelation)
(genl equivalenceRelation binaryPredicate)

;; the three marks an equivalence stands in for, derived so each is a real property the
;; engine enforces exactly as a directly written one is
(implies (and (equivalenceRelation ?p)) (symmetric ?p))
(implies (and (equivalenceRelation ?p)) (transitive ?p))
(implies (and (equivalenceRelation ?p)) (reflexive ?p))

;; ---- the lattice among the relation properties ---------------------------
;; genl and disjoint relate the property predicates directly now that each mark carries
;; its own classification (an algebraic property is one predicate, not a mark and a twin).
;; asymmetric is the strongest of the order properties: it entails both irreflexivity and
;; antisymmetry, so its members are members of those too — a classification, not the
;; :irreflexive / :anti-symmetric property, which only a stored mark sets (so an asymmetric
;; predicate still admits its self tuple).
(genl asymmetric irreflexive)
(genl asymmetric antiSymmetric)

;; and the incompatible pairs: no predicate is both symmetric and asymmetric, both
;; reflexive and irreflexive, or both transitive and antitransitive.  (disjoint symmetric
;; antiSymmetric) is deliberately NOT asserted — equals itself is both symmetric and
;; antisymmetric, so they are not disjoint (docs/equality.md).
(disjoint symmetric asymmetric)
(disjoint reflexive irreflexive)
(disjoint transitive antiTransitive)

(comment typeRelationPredicate
         "(typeRelationPredicate ?predicate) means that ?predicate relates types rather than individuals. So (?predicate T1 T2) is a claim between two categories, and such a ground fact is replaced by one of the relation-pattern meta-predicates to pin down which quantifier reading is meant. The other half of the relationKind metatype.")
(unaryPredicate typeRelationPredicate)
(genl typeRelationPredicate predicate)

(comment typeToInstancePred
         "(typeToInstancePred ?typePredicate ?instancePredicate) means that ?typePredicate and ?instancePredicate are the type-level and instance-level readings of one underlying relation. Declared of partType and partOf, for instance. A link rather than a rule: moving a claim from one level to the other needs a quantifier reading that nothing here fixes.")
(binaryPredicate typeToInstancePred)
(instanceRelationPredicate typeToInstancePred)
(argIsa typeToInstancePred 1 typeRelationPredicate)
(argIsa typeToInstancePred 2 instanceRelationPredicate)

(comment variableArity
         "(variableArity ?predicate) means that ?predicate takes any number of arguments from its declared arity upward. The arity check does not hold it to one number, while its (arity ?predicate N) and N-ary predicate type still state the shape it reads as: lessThan is binary, and (lessThan 1 2 3) is the chain 1 < 2 < 3.")
(unaryPredicate variableArity)

(comment unaryPredicate      "A predicate of one argument: every type (a snake_case unary predicate) and every one-place property such as flies.")
(genl unaryPredicate predicate)
