;; CoreContext — Vaelii's vocabulary context: the engine-interpreted special
;; predicates (types, contexts, rules, metadata, evaluables) and the predicate
;; meta-ontology, each documented via a comment sentex. Schema only — the domain
;; relations, individuals, and facts live in other contexts.
;;
;; The test for belonging here is **grammar, not subject matter**: a term earns a place
;; only if it is vocabulary several upper-spindle members state their content *in*.
;; binaryPredicate, argIsa, genl and unreifiableFunction are grammar — every domain
;; declares its relations, constraints, types and functions with them. dimensionOf and
;; partOfRegion are not: they are *about* something (measurement, space), so they live
;; in the one upper context that owns that subject (kb/upper/MeasureContext.txt,
;; kb/upper/SpaceContext.txt). A term whose only user is a single upper context belongs
;; in that context, whatever interprets it — being read by a prover does not make a term
;; part of the grammar.
;;
;; What that buys is separability, not omission: the starter loads every upper context
;; it finds, but a KB assembled from CoreContext plus the layers it wants carries only
;; the subjects it reasons about, and nothing here has to change to add or drop one.

(comment abduciblePredicate
         "(abduciblePredicate ?predicate) means that a (?predicate …) sentence may be hypothesized. When a backward proof dead-ends on one, abduce may assume it rather than fail. A grant, and the only thing that makes a predicate abducible: an undeclared one is never assumed, since an abducer that will hypothesize anything explains everything and is worth nothing. Read from the asking context's genlContext up-cone rather than universally, so it is a policy of the context that gives it — one theory may be willing to assume what another, reading the same vocabulary, will not.")
(unaryPredicate abduciblePredicate)

(comment agg/avg
         "(agg/avg ?mean ?value ?body) means that ?mean is the mean of the distinct numeric ?value satisfying ?body. No answer at all over an empty ?body, an average over nothing being a division by zero however it is dressed up. A query operator, never stored.")
(ternaryPredicate agg/avg)

(comment agg/count
         "(agg/count ?count ?value ?body) means that ?count is how many distinct ?value satisfy ?body. Distinct by the equality closure, so two names for one thing count once, and 0 over an empty ?body. ?value is projected out and ?count is the only binding produced, so in a rule body the aggregate runs once per grouping the generators bind and yields one ?count each — which is where GROUP BY comes from, with no GROUP BY construct to design. A query operator answered by a prover, never stored: a count is a function of what is believed now, and storing one would put a computed value under truth maintenance with no way to invalidate it.")
(ternaryPredicate agg/count)

(comment agg/max
         "(agg/max ?max ?value ?body) means that ?max is the greatest of the distinct numeric ?value satisfying ?body. No answer at all over an empty ?body, a maximum of nothing being a claim about a group with no members. A query operator, never stored.")
(ternaryPredicate agg/max)

(comment agg/min
         "(agg/min ?min ?value ?body) means that ?min is the least of the distinct numeric ?value satisfying ?body. No answer at all over an empty ?body, a minimum of nothing being a claim about a group with no members. A query operator, never stored.")
(ternaryPredicate agg/min)

(comment agg/sum
         "(agg/sum ?sum ?value ?body) means that ?sum is the total of the distinct numeric ?value satisfying ?body. Measures of one dimension work too: they are normalized, added in base units and rendered back, so a mixed-dimension extent is refused rather than quietly added. 0 over an empty ?body, that being the identity of the reduction. A query operator, never stored.")
(ternaryPredicate agg/sum)

(comment and
         "(and ?antecedent1 ?antecedent2 …) means the conjunction of the antecedents inside an implies rule. A syntactic connective rather than a predicate: it is never asserted on its own, and a rule with a single antecedent needs no and at all.")

(comment argGenl
         "(argGenl ?predicate ?position ?type) means that argument ?position of ?predicate must be a subtype of ?type. The argIsa constraint one level up, for a typeRelationPredicate whose arguments name kinds rather than instances: (argGenl partType 1 physical_object) says the argument names a kind of physical object, where (argIsa partOf 1 physical_object) says it names one. Open-world, so an argument with no place in the genl hierarchy cannot violate it.")
(ternaryPredicate argGenl)

(comment argIsa
         "(argIsa ?predicate ?position ?type) means that argument ?position of ?predicate must be of ?type. Checked through genl transitivity whenever a sentex is asserted, and open-world: an argument whose type is unknown cannot violate it, so this narrows what can be said without demanding that everything be typed.")
(ternaryPredicate argIsa)

(comment interArgIsa
         "(interArgIsa ?predicate ?position1 ?type1 ?position2 ?type2) means that whenever argument ?position1 of ?predicate is a ?type1, argument ?position2 must be a ?type2. The conditional argument constraint, and it says what argIsa cannot: (interArgIsa eats 1 carnivore 2 meat) types the second argument only for the eaters that are carnivores, where (argIsa eats 2 meat) would demand meat of every eater. Open-world in both directions, and they point opposite ways — silence about the trigger argument's type leaves the constraint dormant rather than firing it, while silence about the target argument's is what convicts. Checked through genl transitivity from the writing context, like argIsa, and it entails the same way under assertive argument types.")
(arity interArgIsa 5)
(instanceRelationPredicate interArgIsa)
(argIsa interArgIsa 1 predicate)

(comment arity
         "(arity ?predicate ?n) means that ?predicate takes ?n arguments. One for a unaryPredicate, two for a binaryPredicate, three for a ternaryPredicate. The arity and the predicate-type membership derive each other, so asserting either keeps the whole cycle believed, and retracting the one that was actually asserted collapses it.")
(binaryPredicate arity)
(instanceRelationPredicate arity)
(functional arity)
(argIsa arity 1 predicate)

;; The meta-ontology rules below conclude into the ORDINARY placement — the maximal
;; context that sees the rule and the declaration — and not into a named one.  Every
;; context sees CoreContext, so a declaration made here still concludes here; one made
;; in a context concludes there, where its author put it.  Naming CoreContext would
;; publish it: a private (symmetric myRel) would become vocabulary the whole KB reads,
;; while (has-prop? kb :symmetric myRel ctx) — asked of the same declaration from the
;; same context — answered false.  One KB cannot say both.

;; arity concludes the predicate type
(implies (and (arity ?p 1)) (unaryPredicate ?p))
(implies (and (arity ?p 2)) (binaryPredicate ?p))
(implies (and (arity ?p 3)) (ternaryPredicate ?p))

;; the predicate type concludes the arity
(implies (and (unaryPredicate ?p))   (arity ?p 1))
(implies (and (binaryPredicate ?p))  (arity ?p 2))
(implies (and (ternaryPredicate ?p)) (arity ?p 3))

(comment binaryPredicate     "A predicate of two arguments — a relation such as parentOf.")
(genl binaryPredicate predicate)

(comment comment
         "(comment ?term ?text) means that ?text documents ?term. Ordinary sentexes, so the KB documents itself in its own representation and a comment is queried, retracted and justified like anything else. Written as a signature and a definition, as this one is, so that first clause doubles as the template an English gloss of any (?term …) sentence is composed from; everything after it is prose for a reader and no machine reads it.")
(binaryPredicate comment)
(instanceRelationPredicate comment)

(comment contradicts
         "(contradicts ?sentence (not ?sentence)) means that the two sides are a contradiction among known-true beliefs that could not be resolved. The report form (conflicts kb) returns, so it is the result of a solve rather than a fact anyone asserts.")
(binaryPredicate contradicts)
(instanceRelationPredicate contradicts)

(comment decontextualizedPredicate
         "(decontextualizedPredicate ?predicate) means that every (?predicate …) sentence, asserted or concluded by a rule, is also deduced into UniverseContext. Every context sees UniverseContext, so the fact stops being a claim of one theory and becomes a claim of the KB. The copy is supported by the placement sentex and this declaration together, so retracting either withdraws it. Conceptually the inert rule (implies (?pred . ?args) (ist UniverseContext (?pred . ?args))), implemented in code because it has to run wherever content is stored and not only where it is asserted.")
(unaryPredicate decontextualizedPredicate)

(comment disjoint
         "(disjoint ?type1 ?type2) means that the two types have no common instance. Inherited by subtypes through genl, so declaring it of dog and cat separates every breed from every breed. A hard constraint: a membership that violates it is refused where it is written, and one that only becomes visible from some descendant context is reported by exposed-clashes rather than thrown at whoever wrote the second half.")
(binaryPredicate disjoint)
(typeRelationPredicate disjoint)

(comment disjointMetatype
         "(disjointMetatype ?metatype) means that every pair of ?metatype's member types is disjoint. The clique is consulted rather than stored, so it costs nothing quadratic and retraction reaches all of it: dropping ?metatype releases every pair at once, and dropping one member releases exactly that member's pairs.")
(unaryPredicate disjointMetatype)

(comment evaluate
         "(evaluate ?result ?expression) means that ?result is the value of the symbolic ?expression. So (evaluate ?sum (+ 1 2)) binds ?sum to 3. A safe whitelist over +, -, *, /, mod, min, max, abs and expt rather than clojure eval, computed on demand and never stored.")
(binaryPredicate evaluate)
(instanceRelationPredicate evaluate)

(comment forcedDecontextualizedPredicate
         "(forcedDecontextualizedPredicate ?predicate) means that every (?predicate …) sentence is stored directly in UniverseContext by force. No separate deduction and no justification: the fact's extent simply lives there. Used for genlContext, so the context topology has one canonical home instead of a copy per context.")
(unaryPredicate forcedDecontextualizedPredicate)

(comment functional
         "(functional ?predicate) means that ?predicate maps its first argument to at most one value. A second, different value is not a second fact: two symbols derive (equals V1 V2) and merge, so retracting either fact or the declaration un-merges them, while two non-symbols such as 1980 and 1990 are refused outright, no merge being able to make two numbers one thing. Declared of birthYearOf, for instance.")
(unaryPredicate functional)
(decontextualizedPredicate functional)

(comment functionalPredicate "A binary predicate mapping its first argument to at most one value. Derived from (functional P).")
(genl functionalPredicate binaryPredicate)
(implies (and (functional ?p)) (functionalPredicate ?p))

(comment functionCorrespondingPredicate
         "(functionCorrespondingPredicate ?function ?predicate ?position) means that ?function and ?predicate state the same relationship: ?function maps some arguments to a value exactly when ?predicate holds of those same arguments with the value at ?position. So (functionCorrespondingPredicate MotherFn motherOf 2) makes (MotherFn Muffet) resolve to the Mary of a believed (motherOf Muffet Mary), and, where no mother is known, projects the placeholder constant it mints back onto motherOf so the placeholder still answers questions about who Muffet's mother is. Reading it both ways is what stops the function and the predicate from being one claim the KB can reason with only half of, and a value arriving after its application is equated with the placeholder, so the order the two are asserted in does not decide the answer. ?position is 1-based over ?predicate's arguments and may be omitted, which puts the value last — the shape nearly every correspondence has. Read only for a reifiableFunction, an undeclared function having no application to reify. See docs/nat.md.")
(binaryPredicate functionCorrespondingPredicate)
(variableArity functionCorrespondingPredicate)
(argIsa functionCorrespondingPredicate 2 predicate)

(comment genl
         "(genl ?subtype ?supertype) means that every ?subtype is a ?supertype. Transitive, and cached as a closure rather than run as a rule, since transitivity is the lifeblood of common sense and far too hot to chain. The hierarchy it builds is rooted at thing. Types are unary predicates, so membership is written (dog Muffet) and never (isa Muffet Dog).")
(binaryPredicate genl)
(typeRelationPredicate genl)

(comment reifiableFunction
         "(reifiableFunction ?function) means that ?function denotes an object. A ground application of it is a non-atomic term, and reifies to an opaque nat/-namespaced constant before it reaches the index, so it autoindexes like an atomic symbol while every page still renders it as the expression it was minted from. See docs/nat.md.")
(unaryPredicate reifiableFunction)

(comment unreifiableFunction
         "(unreifiableFunction ?function) means that ?function is evaluated rather than denoting an object. Its application stays structural and is computed by a prover instead of being minted into a constant, so (QuantityFn 5 Meter) keeps its magnitude and its unit readable inside the sentence.")
(unaryPredicate unreifiableFunction)

(comment termOfUnit
         "(termOfUnit ?constant ?expression) means that the reified ?constant denotes the term expression ?expression. The constant-to-expression half of the reified-term map, expression-to-constant being the inverted term index. A quoting predicate: ?expression is a literal payload, not itself reified or type-checked as a term.")

(comment rewriteOf
         "(rewriteOf ?term ?expression) means that the term expression ?expression reifies to the existing ?term rather than to a fresh constant. A quoting predicate in that reading. With a plain symbol in the second position it is instead the equality relation retiring that symbol in favour of the first: the retired spelling stays stored but stops being believed and stops matching, every sentex naming it gains a rewritten twin, and retracting the rewriteOf gives the original back.")

(comment resultIsa
         "(resultIsa ?function ?type) means that the output of the reifiable ?function is an instance of ?type. Transitive, and what places every derived conclusion: a firing lands in the most general contexts that see the rule, the facts and the taxonomy edges it used. Stored in UniverseContext by force, so the topology has one canonical home. A cycle is admitted, mutual visibility being a claim an ontology may fairly make.")

(comment relationKind
         "(relationKind ?predicate) means that ?predicate is one of the two relation kinds. The disjointMetatype grouping instanceRelationPredicate and typeRelationPredicate, so a binary predicate is at most one of the two: it either relates individuals or relates kinds, and letting the readings mix is how a claim about kinds quietly becomes a claim about their members. At most, not exactly — a few predicates are honestly neither, and are left unmarked rather than forced: implies is a connective and relates no things at all, rewriteOf takes either role so long as its two sides agree with each other, resultIsa and resultGenl relate a function to a type and so are mixed, and functionCorrespondingPredicate relates a function to a predicate. (comment argPreserving
         "(argPreserving ?predicate ?position ?relation) means that argument ?position of ?predicate is preserved along the transitive ?relation. A stored (?predicate … W …) then licenses the same claim about A whenever (?relation A W) holds. With genl that is downward inheritance among kinds, so (largerThan dog cat) reaches a golden retriever — and stops at the kinds, since preservation moves an argument and never the level the predicate relates at. (comment transitivePredicate "A binary predicate P with (P a b) and (P b c) implying (P a c). Derived from (transitive P).")

(implies (and (transitive ?p)) (transitivePredicate ?p))
;; genl and genlContext ARE transitive predicates — the engine answers them from cached
;; closures rather than the generic closure prover, which is an implementation choice and
;; not a difference in what they mean.  Saying so is what lets them be named as the
;; relation an argument position is preserved along, like any other transitive relation.
;; The `transitive` *mark* is deliberately not asserted of them: that would route them to
;; the generic prover, which explicitly excludes the two it cannot beat.
;;
;; Stated HERE, below `(genl transitivePredicate binaryPredicate)`, and not up in the
;; `genl` block: a fact asserted before the edge that makes its predicate a subtype does
;; not retroactively fire the rules keyed on the supertype, so `(transitivePredicate
;; genl)` written earlier would leave the arity rule's firing latent — believed all the
;; same, by the other justification, but produced on whatever assert next joined the
;; rule over the extent rather than at load.
(transitivePredicate genl)
(transitivePredicate genlContext)
