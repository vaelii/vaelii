;; CxSize — comparative size, said the two ways it can be said: among *kinds* as
;; stated `typeLargerThan` claims, and between two particular objects as a comparison
;; computed from their measures. Sees the shared upper ontology via CxUniverse;
;; CxWell sees it.
;;
;; The kind-level half is the worked example of argument-position preservation
;; (docs/inherit.md). `typeLargerThan` is declared transitiveInArgInverse along `genl` on both
;; positions, so each claim below reaches every pair of subkinds beneath it: (typeLargerThan
;; mammal insect) answers (typeLargerThan dog ant) with nothing stored about dogs or ants,
;; and (typeLargerThan tree flower) answers (typeLargerThan oak rose). Eleven stated claims, and
;; the closure of what they answer is far larger — which is what makes the declaration
;; worth its keep and also what makes it dangerous, since the inheritance is a claim
;; about the relation and not a fact about the world.
;;
;; Every pair below is between kinds that are **not** genl-related, and that is a
;; requirement rather than a coincidence. Preservation runs downward, so a claim about a
;; kind and one of its own subkinds would license (typeLargerThan K K) — a kind reported
;; larger than itself. (typeLargerThan mammal mouse) is not a fact this ontology can hold,
;; however true it sounds. The requirement is this file's own: (asymmetric typeLargerThan)
;; convicts against a believed opposing claim, and a self tuple has none, so nothing
;; refuses the pair on the way in.
;;
;; The instance-level half moves in the opposite direction: nothing is inherited, and the
;; ordering is read off two measures through the quantity provers rather than off a genl
;; edge. The two halves are deliberately not connected. A dog being a larger kind than a
;; cat does not make this dog heavier than that cat, and a rule bridging them would put a
;; claim about kinds where a claim about things belongs. Both rules are backward-only,
;; like `olderThan`, whose shape they share: an ordering over every pair of measured
;; objects is a great many facts nobody asked for.
;;
;; The measures are not the only route in. `heavierThan` and `tallerThan` are declared
;; transitive (measure.txt), so two *stated* comparisons compose into a third by the
;; closure prover with no weight or height anywhere — which is how a common-sense KB
;; usually knows an elephant outweighs a mouse. The two routes agree where both answer,
;; since a chain of strict measure comparisons is itself a strict measure comparison.

(genlCx CxSize CxUniverse)
(genlCx CxWell CxSize)

(set/backwardRule
  (implies (and (weightOf ?x ?wx) (weightOf ?y ?wy) (quantityGreaterThan ?wx ?wy))
           (heavierThan ?x ?y)))

(typeLargerThan bird insect)

(typeLargerThan cow sheep)

(typeLargerThan dog cat)

(typeLargerThan eagle sparrow)

(typeLargerThan horse dog)

(typeLargerThan lion cat)

(typeLargerThan mammal insect)

(typeLargerThan reptile insect)

(typeLargerThan tree flower)

(typeLargerThan tree grass)

(typeLargerThan wolf fox)

(set/backwardRule
  (implies (and (heightOf ?x ?hx) (heightOf ?y ?hy) (quantityGreaterThan ?hx ?hy))
           (tallerThan ?x ?y)))
