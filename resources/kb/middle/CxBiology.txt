;; CxBiology — defeasible biology theory: birds fly by default except penguins,
;; animals breathe air except fish, living things are alive until they are dead and
;; awake until they are asleep, and warm-bloodedness follows from the class. Sees the
;; shared upper ontology via CxUniverse; CxWell sees it.
;;
;; Four of these rules are the same shape, deliberately: a default with its own
;; exception. The exception's name is what differs, which is the thing worth
;; comparing. `penguin` is one awkward species; `fish` is a whole class, and reaches
;; every kind of fish ever added because matching fans an antecedent out over the
;; subtype closure; `dead` and `asleep` are states that change, so the same machinery
;; that handles a permanent species fact handles a temporary one with no extra
;; vocabulary and no notion of time.

(genlCx CxBiology CxUniverse)
(genlCx CxWell CxBiology)

;; ---- alive until told otherwise -----------------------------------------
;; The negation is derived separately, so death is a positive claim and not merely
;; the failure of a default: an application can query (not (alive X)) and argue from
;; it, where "we never concluded it was alive" supports nothing at all.
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (dead ?x)) (not (alive ?x))))
(exceptWhen (dead ?x)
  (set/defaultRule (set/forwardRule (implies (and (living_thing ?x)) (alive ?x)))))

;; ---- awake until told otherwise ------------------------------------------
;; Sleep is what this theory is willing to assume. A proof that dead-ends on an animal
;; not acting may hypothesize that it is asleep — the one explanation here inexpensive enough
;; to be worth guessing, since an animal is asleep for part of every day and nothing
;; else follows from it. Nothing else is granted: an abducer that will assume anything
;; explains everything and is worth nothing, so each grant is a policy this context
;; states rather than a capability the engine has.
(abducible_predicate asleep)

(set/forwardRule (implies (and (asleep ?x)) (not (awake ?x))))
(exceptWhen (asleep ?x)
  (set/defaultRule (set/forwardRule (implies (and (animal ?x)) (awake ?x)))))

;; ---- air-breathing, excepted by a whole class ----------------------------
(exceptWhen (fish ?x)
  (set/defaultRule (set/forwardRule (implies (and (animal ?x)) (breathes_air ?x)))))

;; ---- what a kind can do, and the kind that cannot -----------------------
;; Flight is a claim about the KIND, so it is said with capabilityType — the type-level
;; reading — stated of bird once and reaching every kind of bird through
;; (transitiveInArg capabilityType 1 genl): crow, eagle and every species added later, with
;; nothing written about any of them.  hasCapability is the instance-level reading and
;; says nothing here; neither implies the other, and the descent below is what crosses
;; between them.
;;
;; The exception is a **more specific claim**, not a rule with an exceptWhen: an
;; inherited default is undercut by a claim about the nearer kind, so stating that
;; penguins do not fly stops the inheritance at penguin and leaves its siblings flying.
;; That is the genl-level counterpart of undercutting a rule, and it needs no exceptWhen
;; because there is no rule to except — the reach is the taxonomy's, not a chainer's.
;; See docs/inherit.md and docs/exceptions.md.
;;
;; The negative is a positive claim that penguins are flightless, which an application
;; can query and argue from, rather than the mere absence of a flight claim.
(capabilityType bird flying)
(not (capabilityType penguin flying))

;; ---- and down to the individuals ----------------------------------------
;; The claim about the kind does not reach its members on its own: "birds fly" and "this
;; bird flies" are different claims, and which one licenses the other is a quantifier
;; reading nothing here fixes (docs/inherit.md, typeToInstancePred).  So the descent is a
;; rule, written once, and it is defeasible for the same reason the kind-level claim is.
;;
;; The exception is written **on the rule** here (exceptWhen), where above it was written
;; as a more specific claim.  Both are undercutting and the difference is what each has
;; to bite on: a rule can be blocked before it concludes, while an inherited claim has no
;; rule to block and is stopped by a nearer claim instead.  For a penguin the rule simply
;; does not conclude, so there is no flight to defeat and nothing to arbitrate.
;; See docs/exceptions.md.
(exceptWhen (penguin ?x)
  (set/defaultRule (set/forwardRule (implies (and (bird ?x)) (hasCapability ?x flying)))))

;; Travelling follows from flying via the capability hierarchy:
;; (transitiveInArgInverse hasCapability 2 genl) + (genl flying travelling) answers
;; (hasCapability ?x travelling) at retrieval for anything that flies.  No forward rule
;; needed: if flying is defeated, the retrieval premise is gone and travelling is
;; unreachable.  A stored forward-rule conclusion was redundant with the hierarchy and
;; created an unnecessary JTMS record.  Removed S261 (2026-09-13).

;; And the negative, which is not the same statement as the blocked default: the rule
;; above declines to conclude for a penguin, while this concludes something — that this
;; particular penguin cannot fly — which an application can query and argue from. The
;; kind-level (not (capabilityType penguin flying)) says it of the kind; this says it of
;; the member, and neither follows from the other for the reason typeToInstancePred's own
;; comment gives.
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (penguin ?x)) (not (hasCapability ?x flying))))

;; ---- where an animal lives is where it is, by default --------------------
;; A default rather than a bare rule: an animal that lives in a burrow is in it now
;; only until something says where it actually is.
(set/defaultRule (set/forwardRule (implies (and (livesIn ?a ?p)) (locatedIn ?a ?p))))

(set/defaultRule (set/forwardRule (implies (and (living_thing ?x)) (mortal ?x))))

;; ---- warm-bloodedness, read off the class --------------------------------
;; Never asserted of any particular animal: the five vertebrate classes are pairwise
;; disjoint, so exactly one of these fires for a typed animal and the KB cannot hold
;; both halves of the contradiction.
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (amphibian ?x)) (not (warm_blooded ?x))))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (bird ?x)) (warm_blooded ?x)))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (fish ?x)) (not (warm_blooded ?x))))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (mammal ?x)) (warm_blooded ?x)))
(set/forwardRule (implies (and (reptile ?x)) (not (warm_blooded ?x))))
