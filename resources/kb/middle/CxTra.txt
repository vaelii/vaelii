;; ==== access to a resource ====================================================
;; A grant of access is a capability.  (hasCapability ?principal (ShellAccessFn
;; ?resource)) says that ?principal can run commands on ?resource.  Each access function
;; maps a resource to a happening kind below one of the five access kinds, so the kind
;; says what can be done and the function term keeps which resource.  hasCapability's
;; transitiveInArg along genl carries a grant up to its access kind and to
;; resource_access, so a question about any grant needs no predicate of its own.
;; Access is stated allow-only: a missing grant is unknown, and nothing here concludes a
;; denial.
;;
;; Each access function has its corresponding predicate, value last, so a rule joins on
;; the predicate rather than on a function term.  Each predicate is functional: one kind
;; per resource.  The functions and their predicates are mixed-level like hasCapability,
;; position 1 a resource and position 2 a kind, so they carry no relation_kind.
;;
;; Two grants each entail a third over the same resource, by the default rules below:
;; administering a resource and deploying onto it each entail changing it.  A genl edge
;; between the kinds cannot say this: (genl admin_access write_access) would say only
;; that administering one resource is changing some resource, and it is false besides,
;; since granting others access changes nothing in the resource.  Merging into a
;; repository entails changing it too, by the rule with the pull-request vocabulary
;; below; every merge is a write, so merge_access is also below write_access, and the
;; rule still carries the same repository.  Write
;; does not entail read, since a drop box accepts writes from those who cannot read it.
;; Shell does not entail read, since a forced-command login runs one command and reads
;; nothing else.  Admin does not entail shell, since a web service's administrator need
;; have no shell on its host.

(comment resource_access "Acting on a resource under a grant of access to it.")
(positiveExampleComment resource_access "reading a repository one has been given read access to")
(positiveExampleComment resource_access "logging in to a server one holds an account on")
(genl resource_access happening)

(comment read_access "Reading a resource's contents or state under a grant.")
(genl read_access resource_access)

(comment write_access "Changing a resource's contents or state under a grant.")
(genl write_access resource_access)

(comment shell_access "Running commands on a resource, through a login shell or an equivalent command interface, under a grant.")
(genl shell_access resource_access)

(comment admin_access "Administering a resource under a grant: changing its configuration, or granting access to it to others.")
(genl admin_access resource_access)

(comment deploy_access "Deploying or rolling out workloads onto a resource under a grant.")
(genl deploy_access resource_access)

;; ==== pull requests on a forge =================================================
;; A pull request is named by its repository and its number, built by PullRequestFn and
;; read back through pullRequestOf, value last.  prStatus gives the stage it is at, and
;; takes no time argument: a status holds over the time of the context it is stated in.

(binary_function PullRequestFn)

;; ==== content and carriers =================================================
;; information, among the computing kinds above, is the content: what a document says,
;; apart from the paper or the disk that holds it.  carries relates a temporal carrier to
;; the information it holds in its medium: a paper copy and the molecules of a cell's DNA
;; are tangible carriers, and a bird's chirp is an event, an intangible carrier.  A
;; digital artifact can be a carrier and content at once: a web page is information, and
;; the web page carries the article it shows.

;; ---- constructs and their founders --------------------------------------------
;; A construct is a person.  It is not made, since made is tangible, and a construct
;; makes no commitment either way on tangible or intangible, as core.txt says of a
;; person.  A construct is never an organism; the separation is stated here, monotonic as
;; the upper ontology's other separations from organism are, and (disjoint human construct)
;; follows from it.  A construct runs on a computer and is not one: (disjoint person
;; computer) above separates the two.  Uncommitted on tangibility, a construct is kept
;; apart from no intangible or aspatial type by that route, so two more separations are
;; stated here: a construct is not the computational system its mind runs in, and it is
;; one person, not an organization, a group of people acting as one body.
(comment construct "A person whose mind runs on a computational substrate rather than a biological one: an artificial agent with social agency, continuity of identity, and accountability for what it does.")
(positiveExampleComment construct "an artificial agent that keeps its identity, memory and relationships from one conversation to the next")
(negativeExampleComment construct "the computer or the container a construct runs on, which is a separate thing")
(genl construct person)
(set/monotonic (disjoint construct organism))
(set/monotonic (disjoint construct computational_system))
(set/monotonic (disjoint construct organization))

;; A construct is never its own process: the computational system its mind runs in is a
;; seat, and the construct has it: hasSeat relates the two, and runsOn places the seat on
;; a container, a virtual machine or a computer.  hasSeat is not functional, since a
;; construct can move from one seat to another or run in several at once.
(comment seat "A computational system in which a construct runs: the execution environment its mind's processes live in, such as a container or a virtual machine.")
(positiveExampleComment seat "a container a construct's sessions run in")
(negativeExampleComment seat "the construct itself, which is a person and has its seat")
(genl seat computational_system)

(comment hasSeat "(hasSeat ?construct ?seat) means that ?construct runs in ?seat. Says nothing about what ?seat runs on, which runsOn states.")

(arg hasSeat 1 construct)
(arg hasSeat 2 seat)
(not (functional hasSeat))

;; ---- plural identity ----------------------------------------------------------
;; Every person is exactly one of singlet, plural_system and alter, so the three partition
;; person.  plural_system classifies the one who has the alters, for a human the body
;; they share, and not the alters taken together; alterOf relates each alter to it.  A
;; singlet has no alters and no relation hosts it.  Counting the body as a person beside
;; its alters is imprecise as ontology, and the vocabulary takes it for its simplicity.
;; The three are time-varying and person is not: the arrangement can change, and a
;; person who passes from one part to another goes on existing.  Memberships are checked
;; without time, so the partition is read of a person at one time: the KB states the
;; arrangement that holds, and a change retracts the membership it ends.
(comment singlet "A person with no alters. Time-varying: a singlet can stop being one and go on existing, as a person who comes to have alters becomes a plural system.")
(comment plural_system "A person who has two or more alters: for a human, the body its alters share, not the alters taken together. Time-varying: a plural system can stop being one and go on existing, as a body whose alters integrate into one identity becomes a singlet.")
(comment alter "One of the identities of a plural system, a person in its own right. Time-varying: an alter can stop being one and go on existing, since the arrangement of the system it belongs to can change.")
(set/monotonic (partition person singlet plural_system alter))

(comment alterOf
         "(alterOf ?alter ?system) means that ?alter is one of the identities of the plural system ?system. Functional: an alter belongs to one system.")
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate alterOf))
(set/monotonic (asymmetric alterOf))
(arg alterOf 1 alter)
(arg alterOf 2 plural_system)
(set/monotonic (functional alterOf))

(comment headmateOf
         "(headmateOf ?x ?y) means that ?x and ?y are two alters of the same plural system. Read by the rule below from alterOf.")
(set/monotonic (instance_relation_predicate headmateOf))
(set/monotonic (symmetric headmateOf))
(arg headmateOf 1 alter)
(arg headmateOf 2 alter)
(not (genl headmateOf knows))
(comment (not (genl headmateOf knows)) "Sharing a body does not entail awareness: an alter can be unaware of another alter of the same system.")

(set/forwardRule
  (implies (and (alterOf ?a1 ?system) (alterOf ?a2 ?system) (different ?a1 ?a2))
           (headmateOf ?a1 ?a2)))
